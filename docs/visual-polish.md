# Visual polish

The release audit covered all 66 landmarks and sampled 10,000 locations. The crafted destinations have distinct silhouettes and materials. The biggest opportunity is to give a few of the longer routes between them a stronger sense of place.

1. **Add rare moving details to barren stretches.** Try a distant seabird pass along a restored coast, a brief steam vent beside a volcanic trail, or a gust across a dune crest. Give each a little breathing room and keep it clear of shrine approaches, routes, and rubbing markers.
2. **Give selected landmarks a clearer first view.** Start with tall buildings where the ordinary approach frames the base and clips the roof. Adjust a local viewpoint or the restored landmark reveal so the silhouette reads without a detour. Keep the reduced-motion view useful.
3. **Keep meadow openings.** The short grass already gives the foreground texture. Use occasional clusters near a path or at a treeline and preserve the quiet gaps. In the dark forest, use subdued accents to clarify the nearest path or bridge while keeping the lantern meaningful.

Compare dormant and restored views in a few seeds. Walk each route at normal speed and check the same scenes on a compact phone and at 1280×800. Verify that recurring details still feel occasional after a long walk and that they do not obscure interactions or add noticeable frame cost.

## First pass

- Restored coasts, volcanic seams, and dune crests can host a short gull pass, steam vent, or sand gust. Each effect uses a pooled renderer, with at least 27 seconds between sightings and a longer pause before the same region can repeat. Placement leaves room around shrines, rail approaches, and rubbings.
- Tokyo Tower, Enoshima Sea Candle, Himeji Castle, Matsumoto Castle, and Osaka Castle have local approach framing. The camera also uses those compositions during restoration and cuts to them in reduced-motion mode.
- Short meadow tufts cluster along verges and clearing edges. The Koyasan approach and the bridges at Shiratani and Iya get a muted lift in their stone or timber, without adding light sources.
